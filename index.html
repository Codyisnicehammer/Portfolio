Independent Research

Current Work

Causal Inference · Sports Economics
Does Transfer Market Spending
Translate to On-Pitch Performance?
A Synthetic Control Analysis.
In Progress
Abstract
This study applies synthetic control methodology and causal inference to examine whether high-volume transfer market investment translates to measurable improvements in league performance for top European football clubs. By constructing counterfactual club trajectories, the analysis isolates the treatment effect of major spending windows — separating genuine performance gains from confounding factors such as managerial changes, squad depth, and competitive context.
Methodology
Synthetic Control Method (SCM), Difference-in-Differences (DiD), panel data regression across 5 top European leagues over a 10-year window.
Data Sources
Transfermarkt transfer records, UEFA/league match outcome databases, squad valuation indices, and managerial tenure data.
Core Equations
Synthetic Control Treatment Effect
Spending Efficiency Ratio
Performance Variance
Key Research Questions
Does a club's league position improve significantly in the seasons following a major transfer window? Is the Spending Efficiency Ratio a reliable predictor of sustained performance, or do returns diminish beyond a spending threshold? How does managerial continuity moderate the relationship between investment and results?
